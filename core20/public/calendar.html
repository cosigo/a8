Work, life, observance and rest

Why the alternation closes cleanly

The 360-day body balances exactly

There are 72 five-day weeks in eight months. Because 72 is even, exactly half are Week A and half are Week B, and the annual alternation returns to its starting orientation after the structured body.

Month rhythm: four months contain 32 business / 13 protected days and four contain 31 business / 14 protected days. The month types alternate because nine weeks is odd. The eight-month body therefore closes at 252 business + 108 protected = 360 days.

Outside the month register

Wilson’s Week · full year-end notes

Five gold element days

After month 111 · 7₈ · NW closes, the month register has completed every possible three-bit state. Wilson’s Week follows the 360-day body as a separate year-end interval:

  • Fire · Water · Earth · Air · Quintessence — all protected Gold Days.
  • Reserved for year-end celebration, holidays, festivals, family, gratitude, civic and religious observance.
  • Five days in a normal year: 360 + 5 = 365.
  • A leap correction may add one additional protected Gold Day: 360 + 6 = 366.
  • The optional sixth day is a leap-extension day; it does not invent a sixth element or a ninth month.
The phrase open-ended means the celebration sits outside the ordinary month/week register. The ordinary form is five Gold Days, with the sixth used only when leap correction requires it.
Possible continuous year-end break: depending on how the ordinary work rhythm meets the year-end boundary, Wilson’s Week may join directly to one or two adjacent non-work days. This can produce a continuous year-end break of 6, 7, or 8 days: five Gold Days plus one or two adjacent non-work days, with the leap-extension Gold Day adding one further day when required. The exact continuous break therefore depends on calendar alignment and leap-year status; Wilson’s Week itself remains the defined five-day interval, or six days when the leap correction is present.

Research status

What this page is — and is not

This is a COSIGO Labs experimental calendar architecture: a proposal for organizing the civil year around an eight-month register, a five-day elemental rhythm and pre-allocated protected time. The images are illustrative presentation artwork; the exact calendar rules are the text and generated tables on this page.

It does not claim that compass directions, planet names or classical elements cause the astronomical year. They are memorable descriptive labels placed on an engineered calendar. The underlying A8 project continues to separate Nature, observation, physical rulers and engineered representation.

The live calendar panel reads Core20's qualified absolute year phase directly and derives the named A8 date from that native year position. The one-shot external orientation record remains upstream and isolated; it never supplies the page's running rate, day number, month, week or element. The formal leap-correction rule remains an open research item.
