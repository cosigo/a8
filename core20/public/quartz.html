2. A8 quartz prism design NOT YET FABRICATED

Why a prism? One single-crystal object can expose microscopic lattice recurrence, macroscopic face geometry, optical-axis orientation, ordinary/extraordinary propagation, spectral dispersion and pulse delay. That makes it a useful convergence artifact — not because any one response proves the length, but because several physical responses can challenge the same realized object.

3. What “space and time in the same crystal” can legitimately mean

Space is physically present as recurrence

The crystal lattice gives repeated translational structure. A8 does not begin by asking how many nanometers separate the recurrences; it begins by identifying a repeatable spatial period and counting/scaling it.

Lₙ = 2ⁿ qₐ

Time appears as dynamic response

The same crystal produces propagation delay, phase accumulation, birefringent splitting, dispersion and electromechanical resonance. Those are time-dependent responses of the same material geometry.

However: the prism must not become circular authority for A8 time. Primary WHEN remains independently recovered from the Jovian system; quartz dynamics are secondary witnesses and measured phenomena.

5. Three-comparator qualification — borrowed architecture, not borrowed tolerance

A · Lattice comparator

Question: what spatial recurrence and crystallographic orientation does this specimen actually have?

  • X-ray diffraction / crystallographic determination of a and c.
  • Map orientation of the a-axis and c-axis.
  • Look for strain, defects, domains, nonuniformity and surface effects.
  • Recover qa at the declared physical state.
OUTPUT A = qₐ + orientation + strain map

B · Optical phase / delay comparator

Question: does integrated propagation through the body agree with the material state and realized thickness?

  • Polarized interferometry / retardance.
  • Ordinary versus extraordinary pulse delay.
  • Optical rotation where appropriate.
  • Repeat versus temperature, pressure and mounting stress.
OUTPUT B = optical path / phase / differential delay

C · Prism geometry / spectrum comparator

Question: does the completed prism produce the angular and spectral response predicted by its geometry, crystal orientation and optical constants?

  • A8 apex and deviation angles.
  • Minimum-deviation mapping.
  • Ordinary / extraordinary spectral branches.
  • Cross-wavelength dispersion fingerprint.
OUTPUT C = A8 angle spectrum + polarization fingerprint
Jovian lesson carried over: do not silently average disagreement away. Preserve each comparator as evidence. A previous qualified baseline may identify which channel changed; an unexplained 2-v-1 split is not automatically “truth by majority.”
Comparator independence boundary: A, B and C are different physical views of the same artifact, but they are not equally independent. Comparator A is primarily crystallographic. Comparators B and C are correlated optical witnesses because both depend on quartz optical properties, orientation and realized geometry. Agreement among B and C therefore strengthens corroboration but must not be counted as two independent votes against A.
Important: the Jovian comparator tolerance A8_TOL = 1/512 does not automatically become the quartz-length tolerance. Length qualification needs its own uncertainty model derived from the instrument, specimen state and repeatability.

6. Full experimental program — beginning to end

Glass-surrogate bench

Begin with an ordinary optical glass prism. Relabel it visually as the A8 Quartz Prism surrogate only for layout and software training.

  • Align source → prism → detector.
  • Verify A8 angular readout on prism apex and beam deviation.
  • Capture a broadband spectrum and monochromatic reference lines.
  • Exercise polarization optics, cameras, photodiodes and timestamp/data acquisition.
  • Prove that the experiment can be repeated without touching the future quartz definition.
Glass can never qualify HOW FAR. It has no long-range crystalline recurrence serving the proposed A8 spatial authority. It is only a bench surrogate.

Select a real α-quartz specimen

  • Use documented single-crystal α-quartz, preferably high-quality synthetic/electronic/optical grade for repeatability.
  • Assign specimen ID and retain provenance.
  • Determine right-/left-handed setting where relevant to optical activity.
  • Record impurities, inclusions, visible defects and prior processing.
  • Do not assume two pieces labeled “quartz” are metrologically identical.

Recover the microscopic spatial recurrence

  • Perform X-ray diffraction or equivalent crystallographic measurement.
  • Recover a-axis and c-axis lattice parameters rather than importing them as immutable constants.
  • Determine crystal orientation and map local lattice variation if possible.
  • Record the environmental state during measurement.
Measured specimen state → qₐ(specimen,state) NOT “quartz always equals one magic number”

Choose the native target geometry

Pick a practical binary ladder level for the first physical prism. 2²⁷qₐ is a useful example because the present comparison is about 66 mm, while A8/31 itself remains 2³¹qₐ.

  • Specify one or more face separations as 2ⁿqₐ targets.
  • Specify prism apex angle in the 512-state A8 angular ruler. An example could use 64 A8 angle units = one eighth-turn; the final angle is an optical design decision, not a Foundation constant.
  • Specify how the a-axis and c-axis lie relative to the prism faces and incoming beam.
  • Record all geometry natively first; legacy dimensions appear only on shop drawings if unavoidable.

Manufacture without pretending perfection

The shop may initially need conventional interferometers, polishers and coordinate tools. That does not automatically invalidate the research, provided those instruments are treated as realization tools, not as the native authority.

  • Cut, lap and polish toward the native recurrence target.
  • Protect against mounting stress and thermal gradients.
  • Do not claim the polished surface terminates on exact plane N.
  • After fabrication, return to the physical comparators; the finished object must earn qualification again.

Establish the three-comparator baseline

  1. Lattice: remeasure qₐ/orientation/strain after machining.
  2. Phase-delay: measure polarization-dependent optical path and delay through known beam paths.
  3. Prism-spectrum: measure deviation/dispersion versus wavelength and polarization using A8 angular coordinates.

Freeze the first mutually consistent state as QUALIFIED BASELINE A. Do not modify coefficients afterward merely to force later agreement.

Deliberate fault / environment experiments

Borrow the Chief Engineer philosophy from the Jovian lab: deliberately perturb the artifact and verify that comparators expose the disturbance.

  • Controlled temperature shift.
  • Controlled mounting stress.
  • Small orientation error.
  • Known surface modification on a sacrificial specimen.
  • Polarization swap.

The goal is not to make all channels agree under every disturbance. The goal is to learn which physical disturbances each channel can detect and how independently they respond.

Native spectrum mapping

Once the quartz geometry is qualified, use the prism as a material/optical instrument rather than merely a length artifact.

  • Record input source and polarization.
  • Measure outgoing spectral deviation in A8 angle units.
  • Resolve ordinary and extraordinary branches where geometry permits.
  • Build a spectrum fingerprint indexed by A8 angle, polarization and intensity.
  • At first, conventional wavelength labels may remain as comparison metadata; the final native goal is wavelength expressed as a ratio to qₐ.

Couple to independently recovered A8 time

Feed the experiment from the same architectural discipline used by the A8 Time Lab: the optical bench receives A8 time/state as an observer input; it must not use the PC wall clock to define the experiment.

  • Record A8 time authority state and Jovian qualification status.
  • Count raw local timing pulses where higher resolution is needed.
  • Keep UTC timestamps only as laboratory convenience metadata.
  • Do not let browser/SSE scheduling become measured propagation jitter — the v5.4.18 lesson applies here too.

Derive the ruler, then speed

Only after repeated specimens and comparator baselines converge should the project consider promoting the length relation.

Candidate ruler: 1 A8/31 := 2³¹ qₐ Native light relation: c_A8 = qualified spatial recurrence / qualified A8 time or, spectrally: c_A8 = λ_A8 × f_A8

The measured result is allowed to be ugly. No attempt should be made to choose a lattice exponent or timing division merely to make light speed numerically neat.

Independent replication

  • Repeat with at least three separately sourced quartz specimens.
  • Repeat after independent remounting/reorientation.
  • Ultimately repeat in an independent laboratory.
  • Compare each comparator channel, not just final derived length.
  • Promote to Foundation only if the recurrence and realization protocol are reproducible without hidden dependence on a legacy length standard.

7. Light spectrum program

Level 1 · A8 angular spectrum

Use white light or a line source and record where colors/lines emerge on the detector. Convert the deviation directly to the 512-turn A8 angle ruler.

full turn = 512 A8 angle units 1 A8 angle unit = 1/512 turn

This can be done immediately with a glass surrogate, then repeated with quartz.

Level 2 · Polarization-resolved quartz fingerprint

Quartz is optically uniaxial; its crystallographic c-axis is the optic axis. Off-axis propagation can separate ordinary and extraordinary responses. Record both rather than collapsing them into one “color angle.”

Level 3 · Lattice-referenced wavelength

The native spatial question for a wavelength is not “how many nanometers?” but:

λ_q = λ / qₐ = quartz a-axis recurrences per optical wavelength

A separate interferometric/diffraction method would be needed to recover this ratio accurately; the prism dispersion curve alone should not be allowed to define its own wavelength scale.

Level 4 · Native spectral identity

A spectral line can eventually be represented by several simultaneous observables:

{ λ/qₐ, cycles/A8-time, A8 prism deviation, polarization branch, relative intensity }

That is much richer than identifying a line by one legacy wavelength number.

8. Three ways to approach a new light-speed measurement

A · Vacuum time-of-flight

Most direct physical route for vacuum c. Establish a vacuum baseline whose endpoints are transferred from a qualified lattice-defined length, send a pulse across it, and measure the flight interval with independently recovered A8 time.

Practical obstacle: a roughly one-A8/31 baseline has a light flight time of only a few nanoseconds in legacy comparison. The current 17-bit daily clock display is far too coarse by itself. A native sub-A8-second timing method or a long/multipass optical path is required.

B · λ × f route

Potentially the cleanest native bridge. For a measurement of vacuum light speed, recover the vacuum wavelength against a lattice-defined spatial reference and recover optical frequency against independent A8 time, then multiply them.

λ_q(vac) = vacuum wavelength / qₐ f_A8 = optical cycles / A8 time c_vac,A8 = λ_q(vac) × f_A8 → qₐ per A8 time

This does not require defining length from c; c emerges from two independently established rulers. If λ is instead measured inside quartz, λ × f yields the corresponding material phase velocity, not vacuum c.

C · Through-quartz propagation

Send ultrashort or modulated light through the crystal and measure ordinary/extraordinary group delay or phase accumulation. This is not vacuum c; it measures propagation in quartz and therefore includes refractive-index, dispersion and polarization effects.

Its value is as an internal material comparator: measured delays can be compared against lattice state, prism thickness and optical model.

Useful published precedent: crystalline-quartz experiments have directly observed splitting of ultrashort pulses into polarized components and measured delay changing with sample thickness. That means the “same crystal gives a spatial body plus a measurable time-domain optical response” idea is physically grounded even though the A8 metrological use remains speculative.

8A. Unitless binary-rung program — same physics, different rulers

A central experimental advantage of ratios is that they survive a change of measurement ruler. A laboratory may record a result in SI, A8, or another coherent system; the numerical labels assigned to the absolute quantities will change, but a dimensionless relationship such as 1 : 2 : 4 does not.

For this program, that makes unitless rung behavior an especially useful bridge between established laboratory work and a future native A8 measurement system. Conventional units may be required by present instruments, papers and fabrication shops, but they need not be imported into the A8 conclusion.

Ln = 2n qa

The proposed specimen ladder therefore asks a simple physical question: if one qualified quartz geometry is doubled, and doubled again, which independently measurable responses also double, which halve, which remain invariant, and which fail to follow a simple binary law?

Normalize first — compare without units

For any measured quantity X on rung n, choose one specimen as the reference and form the dimensionless normalized ratio:

Rn(X) = Xn / X0

If X is expected to scale directly with thickness:

ideal direct ladder: Rn = 2n

If X is expected to scale inversely with thickness:

ideal inverse ladder: Rn = 2−n

This allows laboratories using different absolute rulers to compare the same physical experiment without first arguing over whose unit system is authoritative.

Unitless rung residuals

Instead of asking whether an absolute conventional number is aesthetically clean, measure how closely each adjacent rung follows the predicted physical ratio.

ε↑,n = Xn+1 / (2 Xn) − 1

Use ε↑ for a quantity predicted to double when thickness doubles. Ideal binary scaling gives ε↑ = 0.

ε↓,n = 2 Xn+1 / Xn − 1

Use ε↓ for a quantity predicted to halve when thickness doubles. Ideal binary scaling gives ε↓ = 0.

These residuals are unitless. That is intentional. Temperature, stress, cut, orientation, wavelength, polarization, fabrication error and instrument uncertainty may all move the residual away from zero; the experiment should measure and preserve those departures rather than force agreement.

Published quartz precedent — ratio evidence only

Hirlimann, Morhange and May reported crystalline-quartz pulse-propagation experiments using three specimens whose thicknesses formed an exact 1 : 2 : 4 geometry. Their original paper necessarily reports its measurements in conventional units. Those absolute labels are not imported here as A8 authority.

For this page, the published observations are normalized to the first specimen. The useful comparison is therefore entirely dimensionless:

Mechanical frequency rung

For an appropriate quartz thickness-shear resonator, the standard relation is:

fq = vq / (2 tq)

Therefore, under the same controlled material state, cut and vibration mode:

t → 2t   ⇒   f → f/2

The A8 research interest is not the conventional numerical frequency assigned to either specimen. It is whether independently fabricated and qualified rungs reproduce the unitless frequency sequence 1 : 1/2 : 1/4 : 1/8 ... inside a defensible uncertainty envelope.

Reference: NIST · Linking mass measured by the quartz crystal microbalance . The conventional units used by the reference are laboratory coordinates; the inverse thickness/frequency relationship is the physical feature being carried into this experiment.

From quartz rung to native A8 signal generation

The Sound, Music & Frequency Laboratory has now demonstrated a clean mathematical waveform defined directly from A8 time:

y = A sin(2π fA8 tA8)

Its browser/DAC/speaker output is presently only a downstream prototype transducer. The next architecture question is whether the sampling cadence itself can remain native to A8 rather than beginning from somebody else's conventional audio clock.

SA8 = 2m samples / A8 second
fA8 = 2k cycles / A8 second
Nsamples/cycle = SA8 / fA8 = 2m−k

When both sampling cadence and signal frequency occupy binary rungs, samples per cycle are an exact integer power of two. No conventional second is needed in that native relationship.

OPEN 888 QUESTION: Can a quartz-derived physical oscillator be disciplined by independently recovered A8 WHEN and then drive a DAC or phase accumulator at a native 2m-samples-per-A8-second cadence, without conventional hertz becoming authority anywhere in the defining loop?

The current browser implementation is the 666 prototype layer: A8 defines the frequency and visual waveform, while Web Audio, the operating-system audio stack, DAC and speaker remain conventional downstream machinery. Audible high-frequency artifacts observed there are therefore transducer-path observations, not changes in the native A8 signal definition.

The proposed 888 research layer carries the A8 clock farther into physical signal generation. The working engineering hypothesis is that removing unnecessary asynchronous conversion layers should remove the conversion artifacts observed in the browser proof. That remains a physical experiment to build and test.

Open research program — designed to be divided among laboratories

No laboratory is being asked to accept or validate the entire A8 architecture. Each field can test one narrow physical relationship and publish the unitless result. Existing SI-traceable equipment is welcome as laboratory instrumentation; the experiment simply keeps those units out of the defining A8 conclusion.

8B. Proposed Experiment — 3-D Quartz Spatial Baseline, Light & Propagation OPEN RESEARCH / GRANT PROPOSAL

Objective

Recover a reproducible three-dimensional natural spatial baseline from α-quartz, then observe that same structure through time while light traverses one characterized quartz prism.

The experiment combines measurements that are normally separated: crystallographic structure, pressure or vacuum, temperature, electrical field, measured current, mechanical vibration and optical propagation.

The purpose is not to begin by assuming which effect explains another. The purpose is to measure enough simultaneous relationships that the explanation can emerge from the observations.

A8 Bottom-Up Principle

Don’t begin by inventing an explanation. Measure enough of the relationship that the explanation emerges from it.

1. Recover the crystal in three dimensions

The current a-axis recurrence qa remains a valuable directional witness and supports the active A8/31 candidate:

A8/31 = 231 qa

The expanded experiment additionally recovers the full specimen geometry:

GQ = { a, c, lattice angles, crystallographic orientation, selected d(hkl), strain and specimen geometry }

For exploratory comparison, the conventional hexagonal-cell volume gives:

VQ = (√3 / 2)a²c

and one possible three-dimensional characteristic spatial scale is:

qQ,3D = VQ1/3

This is a candidate construction only. The experiment must determine whether qa, a three-dimensional invariant, or another recovered relationship provides the most stable natural spatial baseline.

2. One prism — one common interaction volume

Use one highly characterized α-quartz prism. The specimen is not assumed perfect. Purity, impurities, defects, handedness, residual strain, mounting, surface preparation and history remain part of the recorded specimen state.

3. Control the physical state while light traverses the prism

Light should remain in the optical path while the same crystal is moved through controlled physical states.

4. Simultaneous observation channels

At each qualified state, record as many of the following as practical around the same specimen and event:

  • Diffraction: a, c, d(hkl), lattice angles, orientation and strain.
  • Prism geometry: incident angle, refracted direction, exit angle and beam deviation.
  • Optical phase: phase accumulation and birefringence.
  • Propagation: group delay and transit interval.
  • Optical spectrum: carrier, polarization branches, line structure and electrically driven sidebands.
  • Electrical: voltage, current, impedance, electrical phase and dissipation.
  • Mechanical: resonant frequency, mode, amplitude and Q.
  • Time: independently recovered A8 timing state plus conventional comparison metadata where useful.

5. Observe the same three-dimensional structure through time

The crystal is not treated as a static lattice number. Its three-dimensional state is observed as it evolves:

GQ(x,y,z,t)

Light traversing that changing structure becomes a probe of the material event:

input light → quartz state(x,y,z,t) → output angle · phase · delay · polarization · spectrum

6. Electrical drive and optical spectrum

A static electric field should first be tested for changes in lattice deformation, refractive behavior, phase, polarization and beam geometry.

A time-varying electric field additionally provides a direct temporal modulation. The outgoing optical spectrum should be monitored for sidebands and redistribution of spectral energy while drive amplitude and drive frequency are varied independently.

Quartz is piezoelectric, so an electrical-frequency sweep may also excite mechanical modes. A particularly valuable observation would be a state where lattice diffraction, mechanical resonance, electrical impedance and optical spectrum change together.

Such correlation should be measured before assigning a preferred explanation.

9. Time and propagation provide the third closure

The final spatial Unit may require more than a spatial average. Independently recovered time supplies another natural witness.

Propagation, oscillation and phase provide the physical interactions through which WHERE, HOW FAR and WHEN are forced to coexist in the same observation.

10. Public realization objective

The long-term purpose is not to require ordinary people to own a synchrotron, interferometer or perfect crystal.

Advanced laboratories may determine residual uncertainty and establish transfer methods. The recovered spatial baseline should ultimately resolve to a practical-scale physical Unit that can be held, compared, subdivided and read directly.

Precision instrumentation refines uncertainty. It does not own the authority.

9. Environmental state is part of the specimen

Quartz does not provide one context-free numerical spacing. Published crystallography shows lattice parameters vary with temperature, and optical properties vary with pressure, temperature, strain and orientation. Therefore an A8 realization must bind every qa measurement to a declared specimen state.

A8 quartz state record = { specimen ID, α-quartz phase, handedness / setting, a-axis / c-axis orientation, temperature state, pressure state, mounting stress, defect / strain map, surface history }
Future dependency: present °C, Pa and nm labels can be used during research as comparison coordinates. A final native A8 length standard should eventually consume a native A8 thermodynamic state definition rather than silently making SI temperature or pressure part of the definition.

11. What would falsify or weaken the quartz-ruler proposal?

Failure modes that matter

  • Specimen-to-specimen qₐ differences cannot be bounded by a practical state protocol.
  • Surface preparation or strain produces macroscopic ambiguity too large for useful realization.
  • The three physical witnesses cannot distinguish geometry error from optical-property error.
  • Independent laboratories require an SI-calibrated length in the defining loop to recover the A8 value.
  • Environmental sensitivity makes the ruler less recoverable than alternative spatial recurrences.

What is not a failure

  • The legacy conversion is ugly.
  • A8/31 is not exactly one legacy meter.
  • Light speed is not a round A8 number.
  • A manufactured prism is imperfect.
  • One comparator occasionally fails — provided the architecture detects and exposes the fault.

14. Example end result — what success would look like

A successful first-generation result would not say “we counted every atom and proved exactly 231 planes.” It would say something closer to:

1. Independent crystallography repeatedly recovers the selected α-quartz a-axis recurrence qₐ for a declared material/environmental state. 2. A macroscopic artifact targeted at Lₙ = 2ⁿqₐ is manufactured. 3. Lattice, optical-phase/delay and prism-spectrum comparators remain mutually consistent with that realization inside an experimentally justified uncertainty envelope, while preserving their different sensitivities and known correlations. 4. Separate specimens reproduce the same recurrence-to-macroscopic relationship. 5. The binary ladder is therefore usable as a recoverable A8 spatial ruler. 6. The practical-scale candidate remains: 1 A8/31 := 2³¹qₐ 7. Independently recovered A8 time is then combined with the qualified ruler to measure, rather than define, light propagation.

16. Future extension — A8 888 / 666 “Rosetta” quartz artifact DEFERRED

Purpose

A future quartz artifact could carry the reconstruction instructions for A8 in the same class of material used to investigate the HOW FAR recurrence. The artifact would not become authority merely because information is written into it; the underlying lattice, orientation and physical state must remain independently characterizable.

directly readable layer + machine-readable binary/octal layer + crystallographic orientation marks + optical verification structures → reconstruction artifact, not definition by inscription

Laser-writing boundary

Laser inscription and femtosecond writing in durable silica-based media are established engineering ideas, but that prior art must not be confused with proof that a particular inscription process preserves a qualified single-crystal α-quartz lattice. Any future A8 Rosetta artifact would require before/after lattice, strain and optical qualification around the written region.

Do not let the inscription corrupt the ruler. Writing technology is a fabrication layer. The native spatial recurrence remains the physical quantity under test.

The working “888 / 666 Rosetta” name is a project label for a future physical reference/reconstruction artifact. Its encoding, symbolism, geometry and archival scheme remain open and are intentionally outside the first quartz-prism qualification experiment.

17. Research anchors

  1. A8 Canonical Bottom-Up Reference v0.4 — internal project reference. Establishes the provisional α-quartz a-axis recurrence, binary ladder and A8/31 = 231qa candidate.
